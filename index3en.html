About Kyrgyzstan

The natural conditions of Kyrgyzstan represent a remarkable combination of diverse landscapes and cultural traditions. A traveler visiting Kyrgyzstan can, within a single day, journey from semi-desert and steppe foothills to mountainous humid forests, alpine meadows, and into the realm of perennial glaciers and snow. Such contrasting journeys allow for an understanding of many geographical questions, wildlife, and ecosystem interaction processes. The Tien Shan Mountains are home to a vast array of biodiversity. In the mountains of Kyrgyzstan, there are approximately 12,000 species of invertebrates, over 75 species of fish, 4 species of amphibians, 37 species of reptiles, 368 species of birds, and 84 species of mammals known today. This diversity of fauna has been preserved thanks to large areas of untouched virgin ecosystems. Kyrgyzstan's geographical location does not provide access to the sea. It is situated in Central Asia, occupying a significant western part of the Tien Shan. The territory covers an area of 199,951 square kilometers. The national territory extends approximately 900 kilometers from east to west and 410 kilometers from north to south. Kyrgyzstan borders China to the east and southeast, Kazakhstan to the north, Uzbekistan to the west, and Tajikistan to the south. One consequence of Stalin's division of Central Asia into five republics is that many ethnic Kyrgyz do not reside in Kyrgyzstan. They remain outside the modern borders in the Pamir region of Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, and Kazakhstan. Three enclaves were created, which are legally part of Kyrgyzstan's territory but are geographically several kilometers away, two in Uzbekistan and one in Tajikistan. The main mountain system is the Tien Shan, one of the most dynamic mountain systems in the world. The southwest part of the country encompasses the Pamirs, another majestic mountain system of Central Asia. Mountains and intermountain valleys cover more than 90% of Kyrgyzstan's territory. The average elevation of Kyrgyzstan's territory is 2,750 meters, ranging from 394 meters in the Fergana Valley to 7,439 meters in the Central Tien Shan (Peak Pobeda). The eastern part of Kyrgyzstan is more humid and temperate due to the influence of Lake Issyk-Kul and its elevation of over 1600 meters. The southwest part is characterized by a hotter, drier climate. The Tien Shan is a source of water resources, serving as a water tower for all of Central Asia and a source of life for the peoples living here. Surrounded by deserts and semi-deserts, the Tien Shan is an essential source of water and a crucial ecological resource.

Life in neighboring regions depends on water formed in the Tien Shan Mountains. Kyrgyzstan's ecology is spared from many environmental problems faced by its Central Asian neighbors. During the Soviet system, there was neither heavy industry nor large-scale cotton production here. Additionally, the economic downturn in the early 1990s led to a weakening of some serious consequences of industrial and agricultural policies, allowing nature to be preserved. Nevertheless, Kyrgyzstan faces serious problems due to inefficient use and pollution of water resources, land degradation, and improper agricultural practices. However, these problems are being addressed through ecotourism, which is currently developing in Kyrgyzstan. Hydro resources: Despite having a large amount of water, Kyrgyzstan's water supply is determined by a post-Soviet agreement on the division of water among the five Central Asian countries. As in Soviet times, Kyrgyzstan has the right to only 25% of the water originating on its territory, but the new agreement allows Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan to use the water that comes to them from Kyrgyzstan without limitation, without compensating the country of origin. Irrigation is extremely wasteful due to outdated and poorly maintained hydraulic infrastructure. About 70% of the country's water supply networks need rehabilitation. Nevertheless, the country has good potential for clean drinking water.

In the northeast of the country lies one of Kyrgyzstan's main attractions—the mountain lake Issyk-Kul, along the shores of which numerous resorts and tourist bases are located. Other large high-mountain lakes popular with tourists include Son-Kul and Chatyr-Kul. But few people know that there are about 3,000 high-mountain magnificent lakes in the mountains of Kyrgyzstan. Flora: Kyrgyzstan is home to 3,786 species of higher flowering plants. Many factors contribute to the richness of Kyrgyzstan's flora. Primarily, it's the diverse physico-geographical conditions. The formation of the plant world of the Tien Shan has also been influenced by ancient glacial environments. According to glaciologists, there have been 26 glaciations in the Tien Shan, which facilitated the penetration of boreal flora into its territory. One of the main reasons for Kyrgyzstan's floristic diversity is its geographical location. The Tien Shan is a powerful mountain system occupying a central position in Asia. It is the intersection point of migration routes for various floras: boreal, Turanian, Mongolian-Manchurian, Japanese-Chinese, Indo-Himalayan, Mediterranean, and others. Such intersection influences the flora of the Tien Shan significantly. Additionally, the modern lithological composition of mountain rocks (soils) contributes to changes in heredity, resulting in an intensive process of plant speciation and form formation. Currently, flora species such as wormwood, onion, willow, feather grass, caragana, and many others are undergoing speciation and form formation. Thus, the combination of physico-geographical factors has led to a very complex floristic situation that has developed in the Tien Shan. Fauna: The animal world of Kyrgyzstan is diverse. The complexity of the geographical environment and its position at the crossroads of zoogeographical regions are the main factors in the formation of fauna. Kyrgyzstan's fauna includes over 500 species of vertebrate animals, including over 50 species of fish, more than 25 species of reptiles, 335 species of birds, 4 species of amphibians, and 86 species of mammals. The fauna of invertebrate animals is not well studied. According to recent data, there are about 4,000 species of insects and mites in the republic. Insects.

In the entomofauna of Kyrgyzstan, there are more than 60 species of dragonflies, 100 species of shield bugs, 6 species of earwigs, 250 species of cicadas, 140 species of aphids, 120 species of thrips, over 86 species of ants, 163 species of leafhoppers, 27 species of burrowing bugs, 178 species of click beetles, 75 species of ground beetles, 15 species of skin beetles, 350 species of leaf beetles, 40 species of weevils, 73 species of longhorn beetles, 31 species of rove beetles, 20 species of pollen beetles, 150 species of sawflies, 33 species of bees, over 200 species of syrphid flies, 27 species of owlet moths, 40 species of coccinellids, 38 species of dung beetles, 86 species of butterflies, 140 species of spiders, and 56 species of mites. Of course, this is far from a complete list of fauna. It's necessary to highlight arthropods, many of which are bloodsuckers and vectors of diseases of animals and humans: ixodid ticks—28 species, argasid ticks—7 species, gamasid ticks (including rhipicephalids)—180 species, sarcoptic mange mites—3 species, trombiculid mites—19 species, lice—12 species, fleas—5 species, sucking lice—120 species, fleas—110 species, mosquitoes—28 species, midges—12 species, biting midges—25 species, flies—56 species, botflies—28 species, horseflies—11 species, bedbugs—1 species, bloodsucking flies—5 species, and gnats—21 species. Fish: Kyrgyzstan is home to over 50 species of fish, including acclimatized ones. The carp family is the most numerous. To increase catches and reduce the number of unwanted fish (roach, bleak, rudd), ichthyologists have introduced Sevan trout, rainbow trout, zander, carp, tench, crucian carp, and ide from various locations into Lake Issyk-Kul. In small water bodies, backwaters, and rivers, particularly in the Chui River floodplain and other water bodies, there are roach, redfin perch, pike, ide, catfish, tench, and barbel. Small unwanted fish accompany them almost everywhere. Amphibians: There are 4 species of amphibians in Kyrgyzstan: green toad, Danube crested newt, Central Asian frog, and lake frog. Reptiles: There are over 25 species of reptiles in Kyrgyzstan. They inhabit various biocenoses. Among them are 2 species of venomous snakes: Pallas's pit viper and steppe viper. The steppe viper mainly inhabits desert and semi-desert biocenoses. Pallas's pit viper is found in various biocenoses (at 3000-3200 meters above sea level). Among reptiles, there are species that are not found above 500 meters, for example, the netted round-headed lizard. Inhabitants of low hot deserts and semi-deserts include: steppe tortoise, steppe agama, Central Asian toad-headed agama, eastern sand boa, striped and spotted racer snakes, and gray monitor lizard.

They don't ascend more than 1200 meters. Alongside them are species—cosmopolitans, inhabiting from lowland plains (500 m) to high-mountain plateaus (3000-3200 m); Pallas's pit viper, water snake, patterned racer snake, quick lizard, worm lizard, steppe viper, gray gecko, yellow-bellied toad, rainbow lizard, desert and Alai sand lizards. Among reptiles, there are typical mountain species—dwellers of high altitudes (1000-3200 m): Nikolai's lizard, eyed lizard, Himalayan agama, agile lizard, banded racer snake, and others. In the last 15-20 years, the number of reptiles has greatly decreased throughout the republic. Birds: There are 335 species of birds in Kyrgyzstan. Among birds, you can see eagles, vultures, hawks, the rare mountain turkey - ular. In various regions of Kyrgyzstan, you can encounter pheasants, gray herons, hoopoes, three-toed woodpeckers, owls, Caspian terns, flamingos, and other birds. Rare birds such as the white stork, golden eagle, bearded vulture, bustard, bustard swan, demoiselle crane, kite, white-tailed eagle, steppe eagle, and black stork are also found. One of the unique birds of Kyrgyzstan is the black grouse. Its distribution is limited to the spruce forests of Issyk-Kul and Kemina. The reasons for its absence in other parts of the republic are still unclear. Typical semi-desert and desert forms include bustards, demoiselle cranes, hoopoes, saxaul sparrows, and black-bellied sandgrouses. Their numbers are decreasing throughout the republic. On Issyk-Kul, Chatyr-Kul, Son-Kul lakes, in different seasons of the year, mallards, gadwalls, common teal, pochards, gray geese, and mountain geese are abundant. Lake Issyk-Kul is particularly interesting, as it is a wintering ground for such unique birds as pink and Dalmatian pelicans, whooper swans, common cranes, and gray geese. Since ancient times, the Kyrgyz have a great affection for falconry birds—golden eagles, goshawks, saker falcons. These birds are easily trained. With their help, hunting is carried out for many game animals and birds (foxes, hares, pheasants, ducks, partridges, etc.). Sporting falconry with hunting birds is of great interest, however, the numbers of eagles and falcons have noticeably declined. The main game birds are pheasants, ulars, quails, rock and bearded partridges, ducks. They are quite common and are objects of sporting hunting. A certain concern is caused by the reduction in the number of nesting birds: mountain goose, bustard. The bustard and demoiselle crane are on the brink of extinction. Mammals: Within the republic, there are 86 species of mammals, including: insectivores—5, bats—16, lagomorphs—4, rodents—33, carnivores—22, and ungulates—6.

Unique animals of Kyrgyzstan include the maral, ibex, and brown bear. Their range encompasses the fir forests of Tenir-Too and the high-mountain valleys of Arpa, Ak-Sai, and Suusamyr. Also inhabiting the mountains are lynxes, wolves, badgers, marmots, martens, and roe deer. The red wolf, a denizen of high altitudes, has left no trace even in scientific collections, yet it is listed in Kyrgyzstan's fauna for this century. However, its existence has not been confirmed. The overall deterioration of the ecological situation in the highlands, coupled with a sharp decline in the numbers of mountain goats and sheep, has also pushed this little-studied species to the brink of extinction. Perhaps only the creation of special reserve areas and national parks can give it a chance of survival. The whim of humans has pushed many animals to the brink of extinction. The intensive hunting of these animals has led to their complete disappearance from many areas, landing them in the Red Book. Of particular interest are the bats that inhabit Kyrgyzstan's caves. Not long ago, colonies of the lesser mouse-eared bat, the long-eared bat, the serotine bat, and others numbered in the thousands, but now colonies of these species have been completely destroyed in some caves. Bats mainly inhabit the southern regions of the country, where fruit crops predominate. Among Kyrgyzstan's natural treasures are the red and gray ground squirrels and Menzbier's marmot. The gray ground squirrel is mainly found in the northern part of the country, while the red ground squirrel is found in the south. A relic species is Menzbier's marmot, which is found in the Chatkal Range. Predatory animals like wolves, foxes, badgers, marmots, and martens are widely distributed, populating almost all mountain ranges and valleys. The development of new lands reduces the habitats of susliks and ground squirrels, resulting in a significant reduction in the numbers of rodents, which are prey for predatory animals (foxes, ferrets, etc.). In intermontane valleys and basins, as lands are developed, populations of many animals decline, leading to the breakup of populations of individual species, sometimes into micro-populations or complete extinction. The development of land, accompanied by irrigation, has a detrimental effect on the numbers of animals that originate from desert and steppe regions. Some animals in Kyrgyzstan are listed in the International Red Book. The Red Book of Kyrgyzstan includes over 90 endangered species of animals.

Legal protection began with the first hunting law, which was adopted around 1930, and by 1979, a list of species requiring protection had been compiled. Many laws have been enacted to protect the diversity of fauna. Special reserves, parks, and nature reserves were established. Kyrgyz national cuisine — the synthesis of the ancient nomadic culture of the Kyrgyz people and the borrowing of dishes and recipes from more than 80 nationalities and ethnic groups living in Kyrgyzstan, as well as from neighboring Uzbekistan and China. The life of nomads consisted of constant movement from place to place in the steppes and mountains, in cold and warm weather, so their food was high in calories and nutritious. The main food of the Kyrgyz was boiled meat (lamb, horse meat, yak) and dairy products (sheep and mare). Modern Kyrgyz cuisine includes traditional dishes of Russian, Dungan, Uzbek, and Korean culinary traditions. Even within one country, there are some differences and preferences in dishes and tastes between the north and south. For example, in the northern regions, the main festive dish is beshbarmak — finely chopped boiled meat with noodles, while in the south, pilaf, apparently borrowed from settled Asian peoples, is more popular. There are differences in tea preferences. In the north, black tea with milk is popular, the tea itself is brewed in the Russian style, and Russian samovars are also popular. In the south, milk is not added to tea, and tea-drinking traditions are similar to Uzbek ones. Green tea is also popular in this region. But in any case, the basis of the national Kyrgyz cuisine is lamb. The meat is placed in a cauldron and cooked in water without adding vegetables. Each meat bone has its purpose and is served to guests individually. Also popular is boiled lamb's head, from which the host of the feast separates different pieces with a sharp knife and offers them to guests according to their status.

Games and cultures

Hunting with golden eagles

The ancient tradition originates from the time of the Mongol invasion of Central Asia, when a good golden eagle and a fast horse held equal value and bestowed authority upon their owner. Despite gradually fading over time, falconry is still popular in some regions of Kyrgyzstan. It is conducted on specially trained horses. To enable the rider to handle the golden eagle, a special device called a "baldaq" is attached to the saddle to support the arm. Typically, they hunt young deer, foxes, and small animals. During the hunt, the golden eagle swoops down and swiftly kills them. Sometimes the birds hunt in pairs, as they would in the wild.


Golden eagles rarely fail when hunting prey; they swiftly kill it, usually by breaking its neck with their powerful talons. The hunting season starts in October and ends in February. Therefore, during the tourist season, the demonstration of this art is limited. However, there are comprehensive hunting games with birds of prey and dogs called "Salburun".


Kok-Boru

The history of this peculiar game dates back centuries. Animal herds grazed outdoors both in winter and summer. Wolves often attacked the livestock. Due to the absence of firearms, herders couldn't deal with the predators on the spot. Therefore, on horseback, they pursued the wolf until it was nearly exhausted, beat it with sticks, stones, picked it up from the ground, and fought over it.



Later, with a more settled way of life, kok-boru transformed into ulak tartyş—a national form of equestrian sport in which riders compete for the carcass of a goat.


Tyiyn enmei

This is one of the variations of dzhigitovka. A rider on horseback must pick up coins from the ground. According to the rules of the game, over a distance of 100 meters in a straight line, coins are scattered into a pit, the depth of which depends on the value of the prize. The more valuable the prize, the deeper the pit, and the fewer chances to pick up the coin.


The victory is awarded to the one who picks up the greatest number of coins in the fewest attempts and completes the distance in the shortest time.


Oodarysh

An ancient form of competition among the Kyrgyz people, oodarysh is open to men aged 19 and older, who are grouped into weight categories, as in any wrestling event. The main difference from regular wrestling is that oodarysh is conducted on horseback. Riders have 15 minutes to pull their opponent off the horse. Even a tumble with the horse is allowed.



If after the initial 15 minutes neither participant manages to defeat the opponent, an additional 5 minutes are given after a three-minute break. If there is still no winner after this, a "draw" is declared or points are counted. A clear victory is awarded to the one who, during the struggle, pulls or knocks the opponent off the horse.


Kyz kuumai

From ancient times, the game was a wedding custom. According to the rules of the game, the bride was given the best horse, and she would start the race first. The groom would then chase after his beloved, proving the sincerity of his intention to marry her. Even if he couldn't catch the girl, the wedding wasn't canceled. The players would switch places, and now the girl chased after the groom, hitting him with a whip.


Currently, this traditional folk game is typically held at celebrations, during jayloo gatherings, or at hippodromes.


Upai

The game of alchiki is known to many. For it, 16-20 sets of bones (48-60 pieces) are used. One bone is designated as the "khan." To distinguish the "khan" from the rest, a larger bone is typically chosen and painted in a bright color.
The first strike is aimed at the "khan." If the player hits the target, they continue the game. In case of failure, the turn passes to another player. Strikes are made using the bones in the same position.


The bone being struck is called "ok" (bullet), and the one being aimed at is called "kiyik" (target). The strike is made using only one finger, typically the index finger. Once the game begins and the "khan" bone is identified, the player holds it in their free hand or sets it aside separately (mixing the "khan" bone with the rest is not allowed).


Archery

This is perhaps the most spectacular national game. Do you remember how at the World Nomad Games Kyrgyz beauties shot arrows, pulling the bowstring with their toes? If in the past, bows were used for hunting and protecting homes, now they are used for entertainment.


Mounted archers of the great conquerors Attila and Genghis Khan instilled panic and routed the armies of the greatest empires of that time—Rome, China, the Germans, and the Arabs. Many competitions are organized for archery. "Zhaa tartyş" is one of the common ones. The rules of the game are simple: in each race, riders must hit three targets—facing them, a target to the side, and one turned away from the rider. In the last case, the rider is forced to shoot behind their back.
